§ 01
Purpose & Why This Guideline Exists
Setting the institutional standard from day one.

Inadvertent perioperative hypothermia affects up to 90% of unprotected patients. In paediatrics, the consequences are not just common — they can be catastrophic. This CPG exists to make prevention systematic, not accidental.

This guideline establishes IMC's institutional standard for preventing, monitoring, and managing inadvertent perioperative hypothermia (IPH) in every paediatric patient aged 0–18 years — across every OR, NORA location, PACU, and day procedure setting we operate. It is a prospective, operational protocol with seven core objectives:

Define IMC temperature thresholds Age-stratified prevention bundles Monitoring standards Tiered escalation algorithm NORA-specific adaptations PACU & discharge criteria Governance-level audit KPIs

Evidence hierarchy: paediatric-specific RCTs → Cochrane systematic reviews → NICE / APAGBI / AORN / ASA guidelines → adult RCTs (extrapolated, explicitly labelled) → expert consensus.

Before any patient enters our care, the question is no longer "did hypothermia happen?" — it is "did we do everything the evidence says prevents it?"

§ 02
Scope & Applicability — Who and Where
No setting is exempt. No patient group is excluded.

If we are giving anaesthesia or sedation to a child at IMC, this guideline applies. Full stop.

Operating TheatrePACUDay Procedure Unit MRI SuiteInterventional RadiologyCardiac Cath Lab Endoscopy / GI SuitePerioperative Transport

All age subgroups are included — neonates (0–28 days), infants (1–12 months), young children (1–5 years), older children (6–12 years), and adolescents (13–18 years).

Key Rule — DPU DischargeTemperature ≥ 36.5°C is a mandatory discharge gate for every DPU patient. No fast-tracking exceptions. Exclusions: intentional/therapeutic hypothermia, CPB, ECMO, NICU-specific protocols.

A warm child in the OR who becomes cold in the corridor or the MRI suite has not been protected. Every link in the chain must hold.

§ 04
Why Children Are Different — Physiology & Evidence
The science behind every recommendation in this guideline.

We are not managing small adults. Paediatric thermoregulatory physiology is fundamentally different — and in neonates, frighteningly fragile.

IPH affects 50–90% of unprotected patients. Consequences of untreated IPH include a 2–3-fold increase in surgical site infection, increased blood loss and coagulopathy, prolonged neuromuscular blockade, cardiac arrhythmias below 35°C, and in neonates — hypoglycaemia, apnoea, and metabolic decompensation.

Why Neonates Are Most Vulnerable
  • Surface area-to-mass ratio ~3× adults → disproportionate radiant & convective loss
  • Minimal subcutaneous fat — poor insulation
  • No effective shivering until ~6 months (Bissonnette & Sessler 1992)
  • Brown adipose tissue thermogenesis suppressed by volatile agents
  • Limited glycogen stores → hypoglycaemia risk under thermal stress
Anaesthesia-Specific Impairment
  • GA widens interthreshold band from ~0.2°C to ~2–4°C — vasoconstriction and shivering abolished (Sessler 2008)
  • Redistribution hypothermia: 1.0–1.5°C fall within first 30–45 min — PREVENTED by prewarming
  • Neuraxial/regional blockade removes vasoconstriction in blocked segments
  • Propofol, opioids, benzodiazepines each reduce thermoregulatory thresholds

Four mechanisms of heat loss:

Radiation ~40% — high SA:mass ratio Convection ~30% — laminar airflow, wet prep Evaporation ~15% — high minute ventilation in infants Conduction ~15% — cold table, cold IV fluids
References: Ref 12 — Kurz et al. NEJM 1996 · Ref 13 — Schmied et al. Lancet 1996 · Ref 7 — Sessler. Lancet 2016 · Ref 16 — Bissonnette & Sessler 1992

The physiology tells us this: our patients will get cold if we do nothing. The only question is how quickly — and whether we acted before it happened.

§ 05
Roles & Responsibilities — Accountability is Shared
Good intentions are not enough. This requires assigned ownership.

Thermal management failure is almost never a knowledge problem. It is a systems and accountability problem. This section names who owns what.

Attending Anaesthesiologist
  • Overall clinical responsibility for the thermal management plan
  • Prescribes prewarming, FAW, monitoring, fluid warming
  • Communicates thermal status at every handover
  • Ensures trainee competency in thermal protocols
Resident / Trainee
  • Implements the plan under attending supervision
  • Records all temperatures per the monitoring protocol
  • Recognises hypothermia and responds per escalation algorithm
  • Escalates promptly — does not manage alone
Perioperative / Scrub Nurse
  • Documents baseline preoperative temperature
  • Prepares, applies, and activates warming devices
  • Confirms and maintains OR temperature at age-specific target
PACU Nurse
  • Temperature within 5 min of PACU arrival — mandatory
  • Continues active warming until ≥ 36.5°C confirmed
  • Applies discharge criteria; escalates as needed
Department Head / Quality LeadQuarterly KPI audit reporting to Anesthesiology Quality Committee. 2-year guideline review cycle. Corrective action plan for persistent non-compliance escalated to Medical Director.

When everyone knows their role, thermal management stops being a reactive rescue — and becomes a coordinated, pre-planned act of patient safety.

§ 06
Risk Stratification — Calibrate the Intensity
Not every patient carries the same risk. Our response should reflect that.

Before every case, a risk tier is assigned. That tier determines the minimum intensity of every thermal management decision that follows.

Eleven evidence-based risk factors assessed at preoperative evaluation — the most powerful: age <1 year, weight <10 kg, procedure >60 min, open body cavity, any NORA location.

Tier 1 — High Risk
Full Bundle + Continuous Monitoring

Neonates · Infants <12m · Weight <10kg · Procedure >2h · Body cavity · NORA · Emergency. FAW + underbody mattress + warmed fluids + heated humidification. OR ≥ 26°C (neonate ≥ 28°C).

Tier 2 — Moderate Risk
Full Bundle, Continuous Monitoring

Age 1–5y · Procedure 30–60 min · Body cavity or regional block · NORA. FAW mandatory. Warmed IV fluids per threshold. Location ≥ 23°C.

Tier 3 — Standard Risk
Core Bundle, Intermittent Monitoring

Age >5y · Procedure <30 min · Peripheral surgery · No regional block · Main OR. Every 15–30 min. FAW applied. Location ≥ 21°C.

ImportantTiers define the minimum intensity — not the maximum. A Tier 3 patient who enters the Action Zone is immediately managed as Tier 2 from that point forward.
References: Ref 16 — Bissonnette & Sessler 1992 · Ref 7 — Sessler. Lancet 2016 · Ref 1 — NICE CG65

Risk stratification turns the question from "should we warm this patient?" into "how aggressively must we warm this patient?" — a better question to start with.

§ 07

A temperature reading you trust, at the right moment, from the right site: that is the difference between early intervention and emergency rescue.

§ 08
The Prevention Bundle — Seven Layers of Protection
Applied as a package — not a menu. The bundle is the intervention.

Cochrane evidence is unambiguous: multimodal bundled warming strategies consistently outperform any single measure applied alone. These seven components are non-negotiable as a package.

8.1 Prewarming [STRONG]
  • FAW blanket ≥ 20 min before induction — mandatory Tier 1
  • Reduces redistribution hypothermia at induction (~2°C benefit, Andrzejowski BJA 2008)
  • Start on arrival to bay — not at OR transfer time
8.2 Ambient Temperature [STRONG]
  • Set ≥ 20 min before patient arrival
  • Neonates ≥ 26–28°C · Infants ≥ 24°C
  • Children ≥ 22°C · Adolescents ≥ 21°C
  • If target unachievable → intensify all other measures
8.3 Forced-Air Warming [STRONG]
  • Most evidence-supported modality. Mandatory Tier 1; strongly recommended Tier 2 & 3
  • Full-body/underbody wrap preferred for neonates
  • Applied before induction and maintained throughout
8.4 Conductive Mattress [RECOMMENDED]
  • Tier 1 & 2 — adjunct to FAW, not substitute
  • Set 37–38°C; protective sheet mandatory
  • Verify function before patient placement
8.5 Warm Blankets [STRONG]
  • All non-surgical areas — no exceptions
  • Head covering MANDATORY for all neonates and infants
  • Reflective/space blankets for transport
8.6 IV Fluid Warming [STRONG]
  • ALL blood products — any volume, any age — mandatory blood warmer
  • Crystalloid >10 mL/kg → in-line warmer
  • Neonates & infants: ALL IV fluids warmed irrespective of volume
  • Irrigation fluids: 37–40°C before use
8.7 Humidified Gases + 8.8 Surgical PrepPaediatric-sized HME for all intubated patients. Heated humidification for neonates <2 kg and GA >2h in infants. Minimise skin exposure time; warm prep solutions in neonates/infants; warm all irrigation to 37–40°C.
References: Ref 5 — Warttig Cochrane 2014 · Ref 6 — Madrid Cochrane 2016 · Ref 9 — Kurz 1993 · Ref 14 — Andrzejowski BJA 2008

Applied consistently, these seven components form the reason we will have a PACU hypothermia incidence below 10% — not by luck, but by design.

§ 09
Escalation Algorithm — When Prevention Has Not Been Enough
Three tiers. Clear thresholds. No ambiguity about what to do next.

Despite our best efforts, temperatures will sometimes fall. The escalation algorithm removes the guesswork. It is not a suggestion — it is a protocol.

36.0–36.4°CAction Zone
Intensify all warming immediately.Confirm FAW at maximum output. Underbody mattress active. IV fluid warmer on. Ambient temp +1–2°C. Monitor every 15 min. BGL all neonates & infants. Notify attending. No improvement in 15–20 min → escalate.
35.0–35.9°CMild Hypothermia
Attending at bedside — mandatory.All Action Zone measures active PLUS: IV warming mandatory for every infusion; replace passive HME with active heated humidification in neonates/infants; notify surgical team; anticipate delayed emergence. BGL all children <5 years. Activate extended PACU plan.
< 35.0°CSignificant — Urgent
NOTIFY ATTENDING IMMEDIATELY.Notify surgical team — suspend elective steps. ECG (arrhythmia risk ↑↑ below 35°C). ABG/VBG. Do NOT extubate until ≥ 35.5°C & haemodynamically stable. <34°C: intensivist consult required. <33°C: paediatric resuscitation + ICU transfer. Incident documentation mandatory.
Glucose — Never Forget at Any LevelAt every escalation level in neonates, infants, and children <5 years: check BGL. Hypothermia and hypoglycaemia co-occur. BGL <2.6 mmol/L → treat per IMC hypoglycaemia protocol immediately.
References: Ref 10 — Sessler et al. 1991 · Ref 15 — Zhao et al. Front Pediatr 2023

The best use of this algorithm is never needing to reach Tier 3. But when we do, having a clear protocol is the difference between a managed crisis and a chaotic one.

§ 10
PACU & Day Procedure Unit — The Finish Line Has a Gate
A patient arriving in PACU is not normothermic by default.

Active management continues from the moment a patient arrives in PACU until two consecutive temperature readings confirm they are normothermic. Temperature is a non-waivable discharge gate.

Core temperature must be documented within 5 minutes of PACU arrival — every patient, every case.

Temperature on ArrivalImmediate ActionsDisposition
≥ 36.5°CPassive warming; warm blanketsProceed to discharge assessment when criteria met
36.0–36.4°C — Action ZoneFAW immediately; notify PACU anaesthesiologist; BGL for neonates/infantsDELAY discharge; monitor every 15 min until ≥ 36.5°C × 2
35.0–35.9°C — Mild HypothermiaFAW max; BGL all <5 years; notify anaesthesiologist immediatelyExtended PACU; HDU if no response at 45 min; DPU discharge CANCELLED
< 35.0°C — SignificantURGENT: full warming; BGL; ABG/VBG; ECG; consultant notificationICU consultation; inpatient admission mandatory; incident report
PACU Discharge Criteria — Non-Waivable≥ 36.5°C on TWO consecutive readings ≥ 15 minutes apart. Temperature stable or trending upward — not declining. No active shivering. BGL normal (mandatory for neonates/infants). Haemodynamically stable.
DPU Same-Day DischargeIdentical criteria apply — no fast-tracking exemptions. Parent/guardian must receive verbal and written education: pallor, cold peripheries, excessive drowsiness, poor feeding in infants.

Temperature is the last gate before a child goes home. It is not a box to tick — it is a safety guarantee.

§ 11
NORA Considerations — Same Standard, Harder Conditions
Remote locations do not reduce our obligations. They require compensatory intensification.

The standard of thermal care in NORA locations must be equivalent to the main OR. Limitations are not an excuse — they are a call to compensate harder.

MRI Suite — Zone 3 & 4

Standard electronic probes & FAW devices PROHIBITED. Fibre-optic MRI-safe probes mandatory. Apply ALL warming before patient enters scanner. Resume FAW immediately on exit. Pre-warm the room.

Interventional Radiology

Procedures 2–4+ hours, restricted access. FAW + underbody mattress for all GA & sedation. Verify compatibility with fluoroscopy. Warmed IV fluids regardless of volume for GA cases.

Cardiac Cath Lab

Cold contrast = significant heat sink — warm to 37°C. Cardiac patients have amplified hypothermia risk. Notify cardiology if core temp <36.0°C. 12-lead ECG standard for all GA & sedation.

Endoscopy / GI Suite

Cold CO₂ insufflation → evaporative/convective loss, especially in infants. Temperature monitoring mandatory for all <2 years. FAW & warm blankets before procedure for all GA and deep sedation.

Perioperative TransportNo patient transported with exposed skin. Warm blankets + reflective wrap before departure. Neonatal inter-unit transfer: pre-warmed transport incubator — mandatory. Thermal status communicated formally at every handover.

The child in the MRI suite is just as vulnerable as the child in Theatre 1. Distance from the main OR is no excuse — only preparation closes the gap.

§ 13
Safety Considerations — The Risks of the Treatment Itself
We warm to prevent harm. We must not cause harm in the process.

Warming devices incorrectly used can cause burns, pressure injuries, and iatrogenic hyperthermia. Every rule in this section exists because someone, somewhere, was harmed.

FAW Burn Prevention
  • NEVER use FAW hose without a compliant blanket — never direct airflow at bare skin
  • Never improvise connections between devices and blankets
  • Avoid sealed draping pockets — trapped heat causes burns at standard temperatures
  • Inspect blanket integrity before every use
  • Document FAW device serial number for traceability
Conductive Mattress Safety
  • Maximum 37–38°C — NEVER exceed 38°C
  • Protective sheet between mattress and patient skin — mandatory
  • Inspect for leaks before patient placement
  • Neonates/infants: monitor pressure areas carefully
Iatrogenic Hyperthermia — Target Range 36.5–37.5°CWhen core temp reaches 37.0°C during active warming: reduce FAW to maintenance — do not overshoot. Core temp >38.0°C in neonate/infant: stop all active warming immediately and assess for infection, drug reaction, or MH. MH features: hyperthermia + masseter spasm + tachycardia + hypercarbia + metabolic acidosis → ACTIVATE MH PROTOCOL IMMEDIATELY.

Vigilance in warming is not just about how cold we prevent patients getting — it is also about how warm we allow them to become.

§ 14
Documentation Requirements — If It Is Not Written, It Did Not Happen
The audit trail of patient safety and the communication link to every person who follows.

Every temperature, every device, every action, every handover — documented with a timestamp. Not because governance requires it, but because the next person caring for the child needs to know.
